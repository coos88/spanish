## Sounds, stress and accent marks

*Spanish is spelled as it sounds. Learn the rules once.*

Five pure vowels: **a e i o u**, always short and clear. **H** is silent. **J** and **g** (before e/i) sound like a rough *h*. **Ñ** sounds like *ny*. **V** sounds like **b**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hola, ¿cómo estás ahora? | Hi, how are you now? |
| El jamón y la gente. | The ham and the people. |
| Hablo español. | I speak Spanish. |

> ℹ In Spain, **c** before e/i and **z** sound like *th* (cena, zumo). In Latin America they sound like *s*.

Stress: a word ending in a **vowel, n or s** is stressed on the second-to-last syllable (**ha**-bla, **ca**-sa). Any other consonant: stress the last syllable (ha-**blar**, ciu-**dad**). An **accent mark** shows an exception (can-**ción**).

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ellos hablan despacio. | They speak slowly. |
| La ciudad es grande. | The city is big. |
| Me gusta esta canción. | I like this song. |

An accent mark can also tell two words apart: **tú** (you) / **tu** (your), **él** (he) / **el** (the), **sí** (yes) / **si** (if), **más** (more), **cómo** (how).

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tú tienes tu libro. | You have your book. |
| Él es el hermano de Ana. | He is Ana's brother. |

> ⚠ Question words always carry an accent: **¿Cómo? ¿Qué? ¿Dónde? ¿Cuándo?**

## Gender and articles

*Every noun is masculine or feminine. Learn the article with the word.*

Every noun is **masculine** or **feminine**. Words ending in **-o** are usually masculine (**el** libro), words in **-a** usually feminine (**la** casa). Plural: **los** / **las**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El libro es nuevo. | The book is new. |
| La casa es grande. | The house is big. |
| Los amigos están aquí. | The friends are here. |

Endings **-ción, -sión, -dad** are feminine; **-or** is masculine. Exceptions to learn: **el día**, **el mapa**, **la mano**. Feminine nouns **beginning with a stressed a** use *el*: **el agua**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La ciudad es bonita. | The city is pretty. |
| Hoy es un buen día. | Today is a good day. |
| El agua está fría. | The water is cold. |

> ⚠ Learn the article with every noun: **la mano**, not just *mano*.

"A / some": **un** (m), **una** (f), **unos** (m pl), **unas** (f pl).

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo un hermano. | I have a brother. |
| Tengo una hermana. | I have a sister. |
| Hay unos libros en la mesa. | There are some books on the table. |

## Plurals and adjectives

*Adjectives agree with the noun and follow it.*

Plurals: vowel + **-s** (casa → casas); consonant + **-es** (ciudad → ciudades); **-z** → **-ces** (lápiz → lápices).

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Las casas son bonitas. | The houses are pretty. |
| Hay dos ciudades grandes. | There are two big cities. |
| Tengo tres lápices. | I have three pencils. |

Adjectives **agree** in gender and number and usually come **after** the noun: **un coche rojo, una casa roja, unos coches rojos**. Adjectives in **-e** or most consonants don't change for gender (grande, azul), except nationalities: **holandés / holandesa**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo un coche rojo. | I have a red car. |
| Tengo una casa roja. | I have a red house. |
| Ella es una mujer inteligente. | She is an intelligent woman. |

> ⚠ Not *una blanca casa*: the adjective follows the noun.

## Present tense and tú / usted

*Regular verbs: drop -ar/-er/-ir and add the endings.*

Drop **-ar / -er / -ir**, add endings. **-ar**: -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an. **-er**: -o, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en. **-ir**: -o, -es, -e, -imos, -ís, -en.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hablo español. | I speak Spanish. |
| Comemos a las dos. | We eat at two. |
| Ellos viven en Madrid. | They live in Madrid. |

> ⚠ Skip the pronoun: *Hablo español*, not *Yo hablo español* (unless you contrast).

**tú** is informal (friends, family, peers). **usted** is formal (strangers, older people) and uses the **he/she** verb form. Plural *you*: **vosotros** (informal, Spain) / **ustedes**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cómo estás, Ana? | How are you, Ana? |
| ¿Cómo está usted, señora? | How are you, madam? |
| ¿Vive usted aquí? | Do you live here? |

> ℹ In Latin America *vosotros* isn't used: everyone says *ustedes*.

### Dialogue: Meeting someone

**A:** ¡Hola! ¿Cómo te llamas?  
*Hi! What's your name?*

**B:** Me llamo Luis. ¿Y tú?  
*My name is Luis. And you?*

**A:** Me llamo Ana. Mucho gusto.  
*I'm Ana. Nice to meet you.*

**B:** Encantado. ¿De dónde eres?  
*Pleased to meet you. Where are you from?*

**A:** Soy de Sevilla. ¿Y tú?  
*I'm from Seville. And you?*

**B:** Soy de los Países Bajos, pero vivo en Madrid.  
*I'm from the Netherlands, but I live in Madrid.*

**A:** ¡Qué bien! ¿Hablas español?  
*Great! Do you speak Spanish?*

**B:** Un poco. Más despacio, por favor.  
*A little. More slowly, please.*

## ser vs estar

*Two verbs for "to be".*

**ser** = what something *is*: identity, origin, profession, characteristics, time. **soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Soy holandés. | I am Dutch. |
| Mi madre es médica. | My mother is a doctor. |
| Son las tres. | It is three o'clock. |

**estar** = how or where something *is*: location and temporary state. **estoy, estás, está, estamos, estáis, están**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Madrid está en España. | Madrid is in Spain. |
| Estoy cansado. | I am tired. |
| El café está frío. | The coffee is cold. |

> ⚠ *Ser aburrido* = to be boring, *estar aburrido* = to be bored. Same adjective, different meaning.

## tener and hay

*Having, ages, feelings, and "there is / there are".*

**tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen.** Spanish uses *tener* for age and feelings: **tengo 30 años, tengo hambre / sed / frío / calor / sueño**. **tener que** + infinitive = must.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo treinta años. | I am thirty years old. |
| Tenemos frío. | We are cold. |
| Tienen que trabajar. | They have to work. |

> ⚠ Never *Soy hambriento*: hunger, thirst and age are things you *have*.

**hay** = there is / there are (one form for both). Use **hay** for something new, **está / están** for something already known.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hay un banco cerca. | There is a bank nearby. |
| ¿Dónde está el banco? | Where is the bank? |
| No hay problema. | No problem. |

## Negation and questions

*Put no before the verb. Question words always take an accent.*

Negative: **no** before the verb. Double negatives are correct: **No tengo nada. No hablo con nadie.** Yes/no question: same words, raised voice, and **¿ ?**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No hablo inglés. | I don't speak English. |
| No tengo nada. | I have nothing. |
| ¿Hablas español? | Do you speak Spanish? |

**¿qué?** what · **¿quién?** who · **¿dónde?** where · **¿cuándo?** when · **¿cómo?** how · **¿por qué?** why (**porque** = because) · **¿cuánto?** how much · **¿cuál?** which.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cómo te llamas? | What's your name? |
| ¿Dónde está el baño? | Where is the bathroom? |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta? | How much does it cost? |

## Numbers, time and days

*Count to 100, tell the time, name the days.*

**11** once · **12** doce · **15** quince · **16** dieciséis · **20** veinte · **21** veintiuno · **30** treinta · **40** cuarenta · **50** cincuenta · **100** cien. From 31 up: tens + **y** + unit, as separate words (treinta y uno).

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo treinta años. | I am thirty. |
| Son cuarenta y cinco euros. | That's forty-five euros. |
| Tengo veintiún años. | I am twenty-one. |

> ℹ **uno** becomes **un** before a masculine noun: *un euro*, *veintiún años*.

Time: **Es la una.** / **Son las dos, las tres...** Then **y cuarto**, **y media**, **menos cuarto**. Days are lowercase: lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es la una. | It's one o'clock. |
| Son las tres y media. | It's half past three. |
| Son las ocho menos cuarto. | It's a quarter to eight. |

## gustar: liking things

*The thing you like is the subject; the verb agrees with it.*

Literally "it is pleasing to me". **me, te, le, nos, os, les** + **gusta** (one thing or a verb) / **gustan** (several things).

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me gusta el café. | I like coffee. |
| Me gustan los gatos. | I like cats. |
| ¿Te gusta bailar? | Do you like dancing? |

> ⚠ Not *Yo gusto el café*. The thing liked is the subject.

Negative: **no** goes first. Similar verbs: **encantar** (love), **interesar**. **No me gusta la sopa. Me encanta el chocolate.**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No nos gusta la sopa. | We don't like soup. |
| A ella le gusta la ciudad. | She likes the city. |

### Dialogue: At a café

**Camarero:** Buenas tardes. ¿Qué desea?  
*Good afternoon. What would you like?*

**Cliente:** Quisiera un café con leche, por favor.  
*I'd like a coffee with milk, please.*

**Camarero:** ¿Algo más?  
*Anything else?*

**Cliente:** Sí, un zumo de naranja.  
*Yes, an orange juice.*

**Camarero:** Muy bien. Son tres euros.  
*Very good. That's three euros.*

**Cliente:** ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?  
*Can I pay by card?*

**Camarero:** Sí, claro.  
*Yes, of course.*

**Cliente:** Gracias. Hasta luego.  
*Thanks. See you later.*

## Possessives and little words

*my, your, our, and the prepositions you need to say where things are.*

**mi** (my), **tu** (your), **su** (his / her / your formal / their), **nuestro/a** (our), **vuestro/a** (your pl). They agree with the *thing owned*, not the owner. Add **-s** for plurals: **mis amigos, tus libros**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Este es mi hermano. | This is my brother. |
| ¿Dónde está tu casa? | Where is your house? |
| Es nuestra casa. | It is our house. |

> ⚠ **su** can mean his, her, your (formal) or their. Context decides.

**en** in/on/at · **de** of/from · **a** to · **con** with · **sin** without · **para** for · **cerca de** near · **lejos de** far from. **a + el = al**, **de + el = del**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Vivo en Madrid. | I live in Madrid. |
| Un café con leche, por favor. | A coffee with milk, please. |
| El banco está cerca de aquí. | The bank is near here. |
| Voy al banco. | I'm going to the bank. |

### Dialogue: Asking the way

**Turista:** Perdone, ¿dónde está la estación?  
*Excuse me, where is the station?*

**Señora:** Siga todo recto.  
*Keep going straight.*

**Turista:** ¿Y después?  
*And then?*

**Señora:** Gire a la derecha. Está allí.  
*Turn right. It's there.*

**Turista:** ¿Está lejos de aquí?  
*Is it far from here?*

**Señora:** No, está a cinco minutos.  
*No, it's five minutes away.*

**Turista:** Muchas gracias.  
*Thank you very much.*

**Señora:** De nada.  
*You're welcome.*
