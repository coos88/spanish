# Spanish in practice: traps for English speakers

## Spain vs Latin America
This repo teaches **Spain** Spanish by default. Both are fully understood everywhere. The big differences:

| | Spain | Latin America |
|---|---|---|
| "you all" | vosotros/as + *-áis/-éis* | ustedes (same verb forms as "they") |
| c, z | *th* sound ("distinción") | *s* sound ("seseo") |
| past tense | *he perdido mi pasaporte* (present perfect, recent) | *perdí mi pasaporte* (simple past) |
| car / juice | coche / zumo | carro (or auto) / jugo |
| ticket | billete | boleto |
| mobile phone | móvil | celular |
| computer | ordenador | computadora |
| to take / catch | coger (**vulgar in much of LatAm!**) | tomar, agarrar |
| tú | tú | tú, or *vos* in Argentina, Uruguay and parts of Central America (different verb forms) |

| Spanish | Not | Actually |
|---|---|---|
| embarazada | embarrassed | pregnant |
| actualmente | actually | currently |
| realizar | realize | carry out (realize = *darse cuenta*) |
| librería | library | bookshop (library = *biblioteca*) |
| constipado | constipated | having a cold |
| éxito | exit | success (exit = *salida*) |
| asistir | assist | attend |
| soportar | support | put up with (support = *apoyar*) |
| sensible | sensible | sensitive (sensible = *sensato*) |
| carpeta | carpet | folder (carpet = *alfombra*) |
| pretender | pretend | intend (pretend = *fingir*) |
| ropa | rope | clothes (rope = *cuerda*) |
| caliente | (only) hot | hot, but *estoy caliente* means "I'm aroused". Say *tengo calor* |
| mayor | mayor | older / bigger (mayor = *alcalde*) |

## Habits to unlearn
- **Don't add subject pronouns.** *Soy holandés*, not *Yo soy holandés*, unless you contrast ("*I* am, *you* aren't").
- **Adjectives follow the noun and agree**: *una casa blanca*, not *una blanca casa*.
- **No "do" support**: *¿Hablas inglés?*, not *¿Haces tú hablar inglés?*. *No hablo*, not *no hago hablar*.
- **Double negatives are normal**: *No quiero nada.*
- **Days, months, languages, nationalities are lowercase**: *lunes, octubre, español, holandés*.
- **Age, hunger, thirst use *tener***: *Tengo hambre*, never *Soy hambriento*.
- **Weather uses *hacer***: *Hace frío / calor / sol / viento*. But *llueve, nieva* (verbs), *está nublado*.
- **Verbs after prepositions are infinitives**: *antes de comer*, *para ir*, *sin hablar*. Not "-ing".
- **"I like it" is backwards**: *Me gusta*. See *gustar* in the cheat sheet.
- **Plural for "some" nouns**: *las gafas* (glasses), *los pantalones* (trousers), *las vacaciones* (holiday).
- **Reflexive daily routine**: Spanish says *me levanto, me ducho, me acuesto* (literally "I raise myself, I shower myself"). English just says "I get up".
- **Dates**: *el 11 de octubre*, written 11/10 (day first).
- **Time uses 24h in writing**, but speech often uses 12h + *de la mañana / tarde / noche*.
- **Money**: Spain uses a comma for decimals and a dot for thousands: *1.234,56 €*.

## Politeness and register
- **tú / usted**: if you're not sure, use *usted* with strangers and older people. If they say *tutéame* ("use tú with me"), switch.
- Use **por favor** and **gracias** freely, but Spaniards often skip them in bars where tone carries politeness. A friendly *Hola, buenas* when entering a shop is expected.
- **Buenos días** until lunch (about 2pm), **buenas tardes** until it gets dark (in Spain: until about 8-9pm), then **buenas noches**.
- **Buenas** alone works as a casual greeting any time of day.

## Meal times (Spain)
Spain eats later than most: breakfast 7-9, a mid-morning snack, **lunch (la comida) 2-4pm**, **dinner (la cena) 9-11pm**. Restaurants often don't open for dinner before 8:30pm. *El menú del día* is a cheap three-course weekday lunch.

## Surviving a real conversation
- *Más despacio, por favor.* Always works.
- *¿Cómo se dice «...» en español?* Ask for the word instead of freezing.
- Describe around the word you don't know: *la cosa para abrir botellas* (the thing to open bottles).
- Say *vale* (Spain) / *ok* / *claro* a lot: they mean "OK", "sure", "of course".
- Don't translate word-by-word from English. Learn **whole phrases** (see [PHRASES.md](PHRASES.md)).
- Listen daily, even if you understand 10%. Spanish pronunciation is regular, so reading along with audio is a fast way to train your ear. The app's *Hear it → English* quiz is built for this.

## How to study with this repo (15 min/day)
1. 5 min: flashcards in the app (only what's due).
2. 5 min: one quiz round, alternate *hear it* and *type it*.
3. 5 min: a verb drill, or read one section of the [cheat sheet](CHEATSHEET.md) and then do the matching [exercise](exercises/).
4. Once a week: repeat one weak exercise from scratch without looking.
